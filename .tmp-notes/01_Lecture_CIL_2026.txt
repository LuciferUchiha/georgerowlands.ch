Linear Compositionality
Composing of linear maps ⇓matrix multiplication
F(x) = Wx,
G(z) = Vz
(G ↘F)(x) = G(F(x)) = V(Wx) = (VW)x
(simply follows from associativity of matrix product)
Does it make sense to compose linear functions for F or G?
No, if the goal is to increase expressivity (or modeling power)!

Rank Constraint
Q: What is the structural constraint on the reconstruction map in
the autoencoder, i.e. how can we characterize P = VW?
Note that we want the inner dimension m ↗n. It is clear that
rank(P) = min{rank(V), rank(W)} ′ min{n, m} ↓= m
Here, the rank of a matrix (or its linear map) is deﬁned as
rank(A) = dim(im(A))
where the image (or range) is the linear span of the columns of A.

===== PAGE 41 =====
38/42
Rank Constraint
The reconstruction map of a linear autoencoder is of rank
less or equal to m. The bottleneck layer constitutes a rank
constraint.
Note that the rank constraint need not to refer back to the
factored expression P = VW. The latter is only a speciﬁc
representation, which encodes the rank constraint into the
parameterization.
