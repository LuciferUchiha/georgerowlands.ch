===== PAGE 46 =====
46/51
Why This Fixes GAN Training
Three beneﬁts, each tied to a result.
1. No vanishing gradients.
A discriminator saturates ⇐ﬂat, useless gradient.
A Lipschitz-constrained critic cannot saturate – gradient stays
clean everywhere.
2. Informative loss.
A near-optimal critic’s loss is an estimate of the Wasserstein
distance.
⇐the loss curve correlates with sample quality.
3. Less mode collapse.
JS measures overlap ⇐collapse is a minimum, missing modes are
free.
W measures transport cost ⇐an optimal critic exposes the gap as
a gradient that pushes G outward.
