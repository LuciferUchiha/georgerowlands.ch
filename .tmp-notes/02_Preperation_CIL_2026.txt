Row Rank vs. Column Rank (Part I)
Goal. Show that the row space and column space of A have the
same dimension.
Let A be an m →n matrix and let R be its row–reduced echelon
form. Then R = EA for some invertible matrix E.
↭Row operations do not change the row space.
↭Therefore the nonzero rows of R form a basis for the row
space.
↗
dim(Row(A)) = number of pivot rows of R = r.


===== PAGE 19 =====
19/49
Row Rank vs. Column Rank (Part II)
Key fact. If R = EA with E invertible, then column independence
is preserved:
c1(Ea1) + · · · + cn(Ean) = 0 ≃↗c1a1 + · · · + cnan = 0.
↭Pivot columns of R are linearly independent.
↭By the fact above, the corresponding columns of A are also
independent.
↭Every other column of A is a linear combination of these pivot
columns.
↗
dim(Col(A)) = number of pivot columns of R = r.
↫
dim(Row(A)) = dim(Col(A)) = r.

multiplying v by A simply scales v, then v is the eigenvector of A
